🧏 isl interpreter

This one's still in the workshop

A real-time Indian Sign Language interpreter for mainstream classrooms: camera in, live captions out, no interpreter required to be in the room.

It's early. Right now this means dataset work on INCLUDE (around 4,300 videos across 263 ISL signs), landmark extraction with MediaPipe, and a first LSTM-based sequence model, not a finished tool yet.

Come back for updates, or check the Projects page for the current status.

🧏 How it's built

Camera in, captions out sounds simple. Under the hood it's landmark extraction feeding a sequence model, not a single-frame classifier, because a sign is a short motion, not a still image.

The pipeline

Camera feedwebcam or phone, no special hardware
→
Landmark extractionMediaPipe hand + pose keypoints
→
Sequence modelLSTM over the landmark sequence
→
Sign classificationfixed vocabulary, v1
→
Live captionon-screen text output

Starting scope is deliberately constrained: a vocabulary of 100–200 signs for one classroom subject, trained mainly off the INCLUDE dataset (around 4,300 videos, 263 ISL signs). Pose-based recognition like this tends to hold up better than raw-video approaches when training data is limited, which matters here since ISL has far less existing data and tooling than ASL.
