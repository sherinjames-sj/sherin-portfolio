🧩 how I made this

Who built what? Honestly.

🤖🧑‍💻 AI-assisted. Human-directed.

AI helped me build faster. It didn't decide what I was building.

This portfolio started as a blank page and a slightly ridiculous idea: what if a portfolio felt like a little world instead of a résumé?

I designed the concept, visual language, interactions, characters, navigation, content and overall experience from scratch. The bubbles, animals, colours, layouts, little jokes and even the things that deliberately move or misbehave were all decisions I made.

1

I designed it. I planned the structure, decided what belonged where, wrote and edited the content, designed the visual direction and worked through how someone should actually explore the site.

A lot of time went into the tiny details that aren't immediately obvious. The goal was to make something that felt personal, playful and still credible enough to hire me.

2

AI was part of the process. I used Claude's Artifact tool as part of the development process, working through the portfolio section by section and iterating constantly as the ideas took shape.

I reviewed every version, tested interactions, caught things that felt off, rewrote content, changed designs and kept refining the experience until it matched what I had envisioned.

It definitely wasn't a one-shot prompt.

I was the designer, the art director, the product manager and the very annoying person saying, "no, move that 6px to the left."

3

The same approach went into my AI projects. For Purrfect Match and Brainrot-O-Meter, I designed the features, user experience and interactions first, then used Claude's tools as part of the process of turning those ideas into working applications.

The actual AI functionality uses the Claude API, while things like the games and local Job Hunt Journal are deliberately built without AI.

And the Pond Derby on this site? Plain JavaScript. Zero AI. Ducks doing their own thing.

4

So what actually happened? I decided what to build, how it should feel, what belonged on the page, what needed changing, what wasn't working, and what was good enough to keep.

I tested, rewrote, redesigned, broke things, fixed things and kept iterating until it felt like mine.

And honestly, what did you expect from an MSc in AI graduate? I was never going to leave AI out of this. 🐣 I was just curious how far I could take it.
